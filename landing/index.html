Find your keys

Beacon is a chest-worn prototype for people who are blind or have low vision. Ask for the keys, follow a spoken direction, then listen for stereo ticks as you reach.

Beacon’s camera test

From asking
to reaching.

Beacon is designed for familiar indoor objects its camera has already seen in the current session. The objects need no tags.

  1. Ask for the keys

    Say “Where are my keys?” Beacon matches the request to a supported object, such as keys, a cup, or a bottle.

  2. Follow a spoken direction

    The chest camera estimates where the item is. A short phrase such as “a little left” helps you approach it.

  3. Reach toward the sound

    The ticks pan toward the object and speed up as your hand gets closer. When your hand is close and aligned, the ticks become a steady tone.

The wearable
and the dashboard.

Speech recognition, vision, and sound guidance run on the Raspberry Pi. QNX gives guidance priority so it can keep its timing while vision models run.

Open the team dashboard to replay the camera test. See what Beacon detected, follow its 3D map, and scrub back to the moment it lost the target.

We tested Beacon while wearing blindfolds. Beacon is designed to be used alongside a cane.
