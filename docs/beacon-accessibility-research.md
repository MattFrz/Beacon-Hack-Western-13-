# Research: What actually helps blind and low-vision people in daily indoor life - and where Beacon fits

Background for `docs/nathans-revision.md`. Product, geometry, and QNX contracts live in that file. This brief is the sourced accessibility research behind the product framing.

## Summary

The strongest unmet need in the sources is narrow and unglamorous: **finding, disambiguating, and physically reaching a specific personal item or everyday target without having to wait for a sighted person**, plus knowing when a tool is guessing. Multi-purpose camera apps (Seeing AI, Lookout, Be My Eyes/Be My AI, Envision, VoiceOver camera) are used situationally but fail often: one 7-day diary study with BLV participants in India logged 67 tasks, 41 judged unsuccessful, mostly misrecognition, no feedback, and vague descriptions; object-finding was the largest failure category ([CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107)). BLV participants in other studies report a problem-solving hierarchy - try it themselves, use touch and memory, use digital tools last - and explicitly want tools that *complement* tactile strategies rather than replace them ([Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91755-w)). Beacon's locked product slice (named-object homing, spoken bearing while walking, ticks only during reaching, honest LOST, no tags, no cloud) lines up unusually well with that evidence. Its main product risks are scope claims (safety/navigation/narration), a closed noun vocabulary, and the fact that **no BLV user has validated the audio or the failure cues in this design**.

Source discipline: this brief prioritizes papers with BLV participants and BLV-authored community writing. Where a study used blindfolded sighted participants only, or a vendor's own site, it is labelled.

## Findings

### 1. Wearer jobs, ranked by how often the cited studies show people needing a sighted helper

This is a qualitative ranking inferred from help-seeking frequency, urgency and failure rates in the cited studies - **not a prevalence estimate**.

1. **"Where did I put my specific thing?" (keys, phone, medication, charger, glasses).** In interviews with 16 blind adults, difficulty locating lost, dropped or misplaced objects was described as affecting "nearly every domestic task" with no widely adopted solution; one participant said dropped medication is "like it's gone into a black hole on the white tile floor," and another described a spouse moving something and "no tool can help you with that" ([Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91755-w)). In a four-week diary deployment with four visually impaired adults, participants lost 1–5 items per two-week phase with a mean frustration rating of 4.75/7, and some items were not found for days without asking someone ([Kientz et al., FETCH, ASSETS 2006](https://doi.org/10.1145/1168987.1169006)). BLV iPhone users describe repeatedly needing Find My / AirTag to find keys at home ([blindios.uk, BLV-authored](https://www.blindios.uk/useful-apps/airtags-tag-and-locate-objects-you-might-misplace-or-lose)).
2. **"It fell on the floor / it's on this table - where exactly?"** The India diary study's object-finding tasks were the biggest failure group (20 of 25 unsuccessful), with photo-quality problems users could not perceive - object out of frame, too close, occluded by hand, poor light ([CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107)). Blinded to the object's location, participants in an object-search study resorted to squatting and feeling the floor; they wanted *approximate direction first*, then tactile search, and said foot-based distance accuracy was insufficient ([Journal of Asian Architecture and Building Engineering, 8 participants](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13467581.2026.2709913)).
3. **"Which one of these similar things is mine?"** Small-package localization was extremely difficult for 95% (N=19) of BLV participants in a package study; 85% (N=17) said they usually turn to people with better vision, and 80% (N=16) preferred familiar helpers ([CHI 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.02704)). AirTag Precision Finding's coarse VoiceOver directions ("ahead / behind / left / right") were criticized by a blind reviewer as less accurate than the visual AR compass, with "slight left/right" or clock-face directions requested ([AppleVis, BLV-authored](https://www.applevis.com/blog/apple-airtag-review-simple-setup-use-deliver-super-easy-accurate-finding-misplaced-items)).
4. **"Where is the water / restroom / door in this unfamiliar indoor place?"** In a phone survey of 21 adults with impaired vision, 67% reported "ask someone" as a strategy in unfamiliar buildings; restrooms were the most frequently hard-to-find target (43%), then signage/text (33%), stairs/elevators (33%), doors/doorways (14%) ([PMC survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8957203/)). In a study of 30 visually impaired and 15 sighted people, sighted guides were the primary means of learning unfamiliar spaces, and participants used floor texture, sound, smell and landmarks once trained ([British Journal of Visual Impairment](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/427172/2/JVI833723_Accepted_Manuscript.pdf)). A 125-person study found "ask for directions" the dominant way to find a room for all groups ([ACM TACCESS](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3514255)). BLV users also want *audible/tactile* landmarks such as a water fountain or carpet edge, not generic obstacle chatter ([HRI study, design team with BLV members](https://hcrlab.cs.washington.edu/assets/pdfs/2016/azenkot2016hri.pdf)).
5. **"Get me back to where I just was / to the thing I passed."** NavCog studies recorded missed turns and over-rotations even with beacon infrastructure; participants valued the ability to repeat instructions, and 7 of 10 in one study said semantic announcements raised cognitive load and introduced safety concerns - "If I become distracted by the system announcements, I cannot be aware of the surroundings" ([NavCog](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kkitani/pdf/AGKITA-MHCI16.pdf), [NavCog3](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/2018/01/p270-sato.pdf)). This is evidence for *parsimonious* retrace cues, not for a general indoor navigation product.
6. **"Is this actually what the app says it is?" (verification when the tool is wrong or silent).** In interviews with 26 blind people recruited with the National Federation of the Blind, participants described testing AI in low-risk contexts, cross-referencing apps, and "strategically including sighted people" in high-risk or important cases; one said "if I'm really kind of twinging that something's not right and it's something important, I will go seek out sighted assistance" ([ASSETS 2024 / arxiv full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546)). Human-like, confident phrasing increases overtrust risk; BLV participants wanted uncertainty disclosed but disagreed on numeric percentages ([same study](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546)).
7. **"Do this without me holding a phone or taking my hand off the cane."** Remote human help is not available at the moment of need: 64% of 325 respondents used a remote sighted assistance app at least monthly, but most used it once a week or less, and 69% said preference between human and AI depended on the task ([AFB AccessWorld survey](https://afb.org/aw/fall2024/ai-usage-research)). Be My Eyes users said it was "not especially useful when the hands are busy or while carrying things" ([remote assistance survey poster](https://katrinwolf.info/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/poster02.pdf)). Note the independence nuance from BLV participants: asking for help is not itself a loss of independence; **loss of control** over when and how help arrives is ([CSCW 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3449223)).

### 2. Why existing tools fail at this job

- **Camera apps are snapshot/verification tools, not homing tools.** Object search requires repeated re-framing, and users often cannot tell that the object is out of frame, too close, badly lit, or covered by their own hand; participants were "clueless" when results changed with environment ([CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107)). BLV participants describe the human alternative as a *back-and-forth* ("I can interrogate that description") versus Seeing AI where "you take your picture and you get the description and that's it" ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546)).
- **Hallucination and cultural misfit are not edge cases.** Seeing AI described a participant's culturally significant garment as an animal; another user's white cane was repeatedly described as a "hiking pole" even after correction ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546)). A Be My AI study with 14 BLV interviewees found hallucinations and failures to grasp user intent; participants used sound and spatial memory to re-aim the camera and still cross-checked with people or mental maps ([PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12338113/)). BLV participants in India reported Indian kitchen items and foods misrecognized or omitted ([CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107)). A baked 16–32-noun vocabulary is a *known limitation to disclose*, not a neutral implementation detail.
- **Trust is low because errors are unverifiable and silence is ambiguous.** BLV participants in India rated inaccurate and delayed results as the most common problem and said they felt "clueless" when an app behaved differently in different environments ([CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107)). A blind user's account of a Seeing AI update breaking detection describes switching to Aira/Be My Eyes "while going through my freezer to find something to eat" ([AppleVis, BLV-authored](https://www.applevis.com/forum/ios-ipados/camera-detection-broken-latest-seeing-ai-update)). In the NFB-recruited study, participants did not have a reliable way to check an output non-visually, so they cross-referenced devices or involved a sighted person ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546)).
- **Human helper tools solve a different job and carry privacy/social costs.** BLV participants worried about showing credit cards, medication labels and bystanders to volunteers; several would rather use AI, and trusted paid agents more than volunteers ([SOUPS 2019 poster](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/soups2019posters-akter.pdf), [CHI 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3587281.3587296), [TACCESS](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3517384)). One blind commentator's view is pragmatic: asking for help is fine when it is faster, and the problem is the "enabling/agency" question asked *about* blind people by sighted people ([Chris Hofstader, blind author](https://www.chrishofstader.com/be-my-eyes-and-asking-for-help/)).
- **Tags work well but require tagging and a phone.** AFB's blind reviewer found AirTag + Siri + Precision Finding excellent, but noted the extra holder needed, shorter sound, and lower volume vs Tile ([AFB AccessWorld](https://afb.org/aw/22/6/17588)). A BLV reviewer notes UWB Precision Finding needs specific iPhones, shows approximate room-level location ("it is with you" but not necessarily same room), and cannot tell which floor; calibration delay and weak-signal messages break trust ([AppleVis](https://www.applevis.com/blog/apple-airtag-review-simple-setup-use-deliver-super-easy-accurate-finding-misplaced-items), [blindios.uk](https://www.blindios.uk/useful-apps/airtags-tag-and-locate-objects-you-might-misplace-or-lose), [Mosen At Large transcript with blind hosts](https://mosen.org/malp118transcript/)). Beacon's no-tag stance is a real differentiator - but only for items the camera has actually seen.
- **Navigation apps are not object finders and can overload.** NavCog participants valued repeatable instructions but reported turn errors and cognitive load ([CMU](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kkitani/pdf/AGKITA-MHCI16.pdf), [NavCog3](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/2018/01/p270-sato.pdf)); a 10-blind-participant study of "parsimonious instructions" cut instruction count from 77.2 to 53.8 without hurting travel time, and participants complained that rapid, opposing corrections left them unable to orient ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12682350/)). A review of mobility tech trust found that high information load, erroneous data, overtrust after failure, stigma and privacy all reduce trust ([SAGE journal review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10554181251355426)). WeWALK-type devices drew comments about added complexity and vibration interfering with cane feedback ([arXiv navigation review, includes user comments](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06379)).

### 3. Audio UX: what people will actually follow with a cane in the other hand

- **Speech for coarse, on-demand guidance; non-speech for fine-grained near-field work.** In a 12-BLV-participant CHI 2026 hand-steering study, verbal instructions were faster (7.39 s vs 24.6 s) but sonified guidance had far lower error overall (19.2% vs 39.2%; 4.17% vs 24.6% in the widest condition); preference split 6/6, and the sonification's big failure mode was *losing the sound entirely* when off-axis - "like a guessing game" ([CHI 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790782)). This is a direct argument for Beacon's split: speech on WALK, ticks on REACH, plus an always-findable off-axis cue.
- **Beep repetition rate is the most learnable distance encoding.** In a depth-sonification comparison (28 blindfolded sighted participants - **sighted-researcher-only population**), beep repetition rate gave the best depth estimates and was easiest to remember after a 10-minute break ([IEEE THMS](https://doi.org/10.1109/thms.2023.3265972)). A 47-person micro-guidance study (9 BLV, 38 blindfolded sighted) found alternation/tempo and spatialization useful for 2D manual guidance such as retrieving items from a pantry ([CHI/ASSETS](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3572921.3572929)). Head-referenced spatial audio beat speech-only for tabletop reach time and workload, **but the participants were blindfolded sighted** - treat as directional evidence only ([IEEE SII 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/sii64115.2026.11404455)).
- **Do not flood the walking channel.** A study with 10 blind participants asking about a camera-based aid found preference for speech but for *querying* the system rather than continuous speech, and for keypad-like control rather than voice recognition ([Journal of Visual Impairment & Blindness](https://doi.org/10.1177/0145482x1310700205)). BLV climbers said a five-second description while moving is useless - "Just tell me 'right hand, two o'clock'" - and wanted brief, repeatable, action-timed cues plus explicit uncertainty management ([HoldLight, BLV participants](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13445)). A formative study with 15 PVI and evaluation with 9 PVI found low latency, minimal cognitive load, and hallucination-resistant output were the governing requirements ([arxiv/ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2511.00945)).
- **Keep the ears open.** In the same 15-person formative study, 14/15 wanted non-occluding audio: "a visually impaired person must rely on their ears to perceive the world" and cannot safely walk with in-ear headphones; participants warned against frequent repetitive alerts that mask environmental sound ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2511.00945)). A BLV-authored track study found vibrations hard to perceive while holding a cane or dog, and low-frequency sounds interfered with existing navigation strategies ([ICHI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3234695.3236345)). A hip-vibration belt produced closer path-following but slower travel than audio ([10 blind participants](https://doi.org/10.1109/toh.2015.2409980)); another 12-blind-participant study found speech better in quiet and tactile competitive in street noise ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6930908/)). Cognitive-load EEG work with 8 visually impaired participants found haptic cues less intuitive than audio and device navigation increased working-memory load; **the researchers, not the wearers, authored the interpretation** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7602506/)).
- **Uncertainty should be audible and categorical, not a number by default.** BLV participants in the NFB-recruited study split on numeric confidence: some wanted "10% rattlesnake" or "80% accurate," others said parsing it interrupted their workflow, and one noted the system has no ground truth against which to compute a real percentage ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546)). Other BLV participants preferred hedged phrasing ("I can't be too sure, but based on what I see…") and neutral, factual tone rather than emotional scene interpretation ([diary study](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13469v2)). A BLV-participant study of MLLM hallucinations asked for scenario-specific real-time alerts but not "lengthy voice broadcasts," and preferred tone-based confidence over numeric scores ([UIST 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3746059.3747597)); a separate BLV-inclusive study showed surfacing variation raised detection of unreliable claims 4.9× ([CHI 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3663547.3746393)). A "Calibrated Honesty" design with explicit high/uncertain/withheld tiers raised trust scores with BLV users and cut overconfident wrong assertions from ~11% to ~3% ([CHI 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830398.3830496)). Note that confidence conveyed by speech prosody is perceived accurately by PVI only up to ~1.5× speedup in one survey (151 PVI, 170 sighted) ([W4A](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3677846.3677862)).

### 4. Framing and ethics

- Use **"people who are blind or have low vision"**; avoid "the blind," "visually challenged," "suffering from," "brave," "inspirational," and "blindness as tragedy" ([CNIB inclusive language guidelines](https://www.cnib.ca/sites/default/files/2024-10/24-0432-CNIB_Inclusive%20Language%20Guidelines_ENG_FINAL-s.pdf), [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-communication/inclusive-language-words-to-use-and-avoid-when-writing-about-disability)). There is genuine community disagreement: Be My Eyes' own guide (a **vendor**) prefers "people who are blind or have low vision" and rejects "visually impaired," while Ireland's Voice of Vision Impairment (**BLV-led**) regards "visually impaired" as a legitimate umbrella term and rejects the false distinction between "blind and visually impaired" ([Be My Eyes guide](https://www.bemyeyes.com/blog/be-my-eyes-inclusive-language-guide/), [VVI policy](https://vvi.ie/our-policies/accessible-communications-policy/3-language-and-terminology/)). Respect self-identification.
- "Nothing about us without us" is not a slogan: NFB's own publication says companies rarely come to blind people before designing and marketing products, and warns against overpromising ([Anil Lewis, NFB Braille Monitor](https://nfb.org/sites/default/files/images/nfb/publications/bm/bm21/bm2105/bm210510.htm)); an ACM Interactions piece calls radar-on-cane-style parachute design "almost a meme among blind individuals" and argues for sustained codesign ([ACM Interactions](https://interactions.acm.org/archive/view/january-february-2023/visualization-for-the-blind)). A blind ML researcher makes the same point about access to the tools of development ([University of Washington, blind researcher](https://news.cs.washington.edu/2022/05/09/apple-scholar-in-ai-ml-venkatesh-potluri-advances-artificial-intelligence-for-accessible-ui-design-that-empowers-developers-as-well-as-users/)).
- "Giving sight" and cure framing are specifically objectionable to blind people: in interviews with 17 people with inherited retinal conditions recruited via NFB, participants criticized "curing blindness" and "bringing people who are suffering out of darkness" rhetoric and worried that a cure would reduce investment in accessibility ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6625087/)). Frame Beacon as **access to a specific action**, not as restored vision.
- Beacon's "AI proposes, high-priority thread disposes" architecture is a good *honesty* story only if the wearer-facing claim matches: sensors give a measured bearing; the device says when it no longer has one. BLV participants' demand is not "more sight," it is **controllable, verifiable, non-embarrassing access** ([CSCW 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3449223), [ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546)).

## Recommendations

### A. Where Beacon already matches the evidence

- **Named-object homing, not narration.** Matches the finding that object-finding fails more often than detection and that scene description is used situationally, not continuously ([CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107), [Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91755-w)).
- **No tags.** Removes the pairing/holder/phone/UWB prerequisites that BLV reviewers identify as AirTag friction ([AFB](https://afb.org/aw/22/6/17588), [AppleVis](https://www.applevis.com/blog/apple-airtag-review-simple-setup-use-deliver-super-easy-accurate-finding-misplaced-items)).
- **Walk = short speech, reach = ticks.** Mirrors the speech-for-coarse / sonification-for-fine split, and the "query, don't stream" preference ([CHI 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790782), [JVIB](https://doi.org/10.1177/0145482x1310700205), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2511.00945)).
- **LOST with no invented metres.** Directly answers the hallucination/overtrust and unverifiable-error findings ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [CHI 2026 calibrated honesty](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830398.3830496)).
- **On-device, no camera sent to a stranger.** Addresses documented privacy concerns about human-powered VAT ([SOUPS](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/soups2019posters-akter.pdf), [CHI 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3587281.3587296)).
- **Chest-worn, hands free.** Fits the "hands busy" complaint about remote assistance ([poster survey](https://katrinwolf.info/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/poster02.pdf)), *pending BLV validation*.

### B. What Beacon still misses

1. **No BLV tester has been involved in the locked spec.** This is the top risk. The literature calls parachute design a recurring failure mode and recommends codesign ([ACM Interactions](https://interactions.acm.org/archive/view/january-february-2023/visualization-for-the-blind), [NFB](https://nfb.org/sites/default/files/images/nfb/publications/bm/bm21/bm2105/bm210510.htm)). Say "prototype, not yet validated with BLV wearers" in the pitch.
2. **The noun list is a product limitation, not a detail.** A teachable, personalized object-recognition app reached 83% "helped" in 71 valid runs with 15 BLV participants but still failed on low contrast and similar-looking objects; failures were concentrated where phones lacked LiDAR ([Find My Things, Microsoft Research/CNIB recruitment](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Morrison-Find-My-Things-ASSETS2023.pdf)). Beacon's 16–32 baked labels and "no retraining" must be stated plainly; "keys" must map to a label the detector actually produces. Cultural and personal artifacts are a documented misfit source ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107)).
3. **It does not compete with tactile strategies and should say so.** Participants prefer organization, labels, memory and touch first; digital tools are a last resort and are abandoned when updates break them ([Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91755-w)). Position Beacon as useful when the thing has moved or you are somewhere you do not control - not as a replacement for a consistent home.
4. **Floor-level dropped items may be outside the locked geometry.** The chest camera's claim is tabletop and in-view; a dropped medication on a white tile floor is a cited pain point ([Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91755-w)) and floor search is exactly where BLV participants drop to hands and knees ([object search study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13467581.2026.2709913)). Do not demo a floor drop unless it was actually tested.
5. **No contest/repeat affordance is specified.** BLV users want to question and refine output; the ability to say "not that one / repeat / cancel" is a documented need ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [HoldLight](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13445)). `STOP` exists in the intent set; a "repeat last cue" and "that's the wrong one" response should be explicit wearer-facing behavior.
6. **Headset choice is a safety/acceptability question.** A USB headset is in the spec; 14/15 PVI in one study wanted non-occluding audio ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2511.00945)), and cane users had trouble with body-worn vibration and low-frequency sound ([ICHI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3234695.3236345)). Field-test one-ear, bone-conduction and open-ear options before claiming a form factor is right.
7. **"LOST" cue design is unvalidated.** A distinct earcon plus a short spoken "I lost it; stopping" is defensible from the uncertainty literature, but the exact sound, the HOLD duration, and whether a repeated cue annoys or alarms need BLV testing ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [UIST 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3746059.3747597)).
8. **RETURN is not the same as indoor navigation.** NavCog's errors and overload findings say a short, optional retrace is plausible; a general "take me anywhere" claim is not supported ([CMU](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kkitani/pdf/AGKITA-MHCI16.pdf), [NavCog3](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/2018/01/p270-sato.pdf), [NavGraph](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12682350/)).

### C. Audio UX dos and don'ts for a chest-worn homing device

**Dos**

- Query-driven and short: "Keys. About two metres ahead, a little left." Repeat roughly every 3 s only while walking, and stop when the target is acquired. Query-based speech and parsimonious instructions are supported by blind-participant studies ([JVIB](https://doi.org/10.1177/0145482x1310700205), [NavGraph](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12682350/), [HoldLight](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13445)).
- Use **tick rate** for hand-to-target distance and stereo pan for lateral direction; reserve a continuous tone for near-contact. Repetition rate is the most memorable depth encoding and 2D micro-guidance benefits from alternation and spatialization ([IEEE THMS, sighted-blindfolded only](https://doi.org/10.1109/thms.2023.3265972), [micro-guidance](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3572921.3572929), [CHI 2026 hand-steering with 12 BLV](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790782)).
- Give an explicit **off-axis search cue** when the hand is near in range but far in angle; silence when off-axis was the main failure mode in the hand-steering study ([CHI 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790782)).
- Make LOST a *different sound*, then one short sentence with no number: "I lost the target. Stopping." Prefer categorical, hedged language over percentages ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [calibrated honesty](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830398.3830496), [BLV diary](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13469v2)).
- Keep at least one ear open to ambient sound; test bone-conduction or single-ear presentation before locking the USB headset ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2511.00945), [ICHI cane study](https://doi.org/10.1145/3234695.3236345)).
- Add repeat-last-cue, "wrong one," and cancel as first-class voice commands ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [NavCog repeat preference](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kkitani/pdf/AGKITA-MHCI16.pdf)).
- Test speech rate up to ~1.5× with BLV listeners; confidence conveyed by prosody degrades above that ([W4A](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3677846.3677862)).

**Don't**

- Don't narrate the room while walking, and don't turn WALK into a stream of corrections. Cognitive load and loss of environmental awareness are documented costs ([NavCog3](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/2018/01/p270-sato.pdf), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2511.00945), [NavGraph](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12682350/)).
- Don't use vague "a little left" without a distance band or clock-face option when precision matters; BLV users asked for "slight left/right" or "4 o'clock" over coarse quadrants ([AppleVis](https://www.applevis.com/blog/apple-airtag-review-simple-setup-use-deliver-super-easy-accurate-finding-misplaced-items)).
- Don't put guidance on the cane hand or add vibration that competes with cane feedback ([ICHI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3234695.3236345), [arXiv navigation review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06379)).
- Don't make a faster tick mean *error* or *lost* - rate already means *closer*. Use a different timbre/pattern ([CHI 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790782), [micro-guidance](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3572921.3572929)).
- Don't speak metres from stale frames, and don't go silent when perception dies ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107)).
- Don't treat blindfolded-sighted results as equivalent to BLV results; the hand-steering and track studies show meaningful differences between the groups ([CHI 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790782), [ICHI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3234695.3236345)).

### D. Framing: phrases to avoid and better alternatives

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "We give blind people sight" / "see with sound" / "a camera for the blind" | Cure and "restored vision" framing is specifically criticized by blind people ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6625087/)) | "A pocket-size homing cue for a named item: get there and reach it without waiting for a sighted person." |
| "The blind," "the visually impaired," "suffering from," "brave," "inspirational" | Paternalistic or tragedy framing ([CNIB](https://www.cnib.ca/sites/default/files/2024-10/24-0432-CNIB_Inclusive%20Language%20Guidelines_ENG_FINAL-s.pdf), [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-communication/inclusive-language-words-to-use-and-avoid-when-writing-about-disability)) | "People who are blind or have low vision"; ask individuals how they self-identify ([VVI](https://vvi.ie/our-policies/accessible-communications-policy/3-language-and-terminology/)). |
| "Replaces the cane" / "guide dog on a chip" / "keeps you safe" / "navigates stairs, traffic, obstacles" | Safety claims the spec does not demonstrate; cane-related stigma and vibration interference are documented ([ACM Interactions](https://interactions.acm.org/archive/view/january-february-2023/visualization-for-the-blind), [arXiv navigation review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06379)) | "A supplement to the cane and to your own memory and labels. It does not detect obstacles, stairs or traffic." |
| "Seeing AI / Be My Eyes / Aira, but better" | Wrong job: those are description and remote-human tools; privacy, latency and verification complaints are specific ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107), [SOUPS](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/soups2019posters-akter.pdf)) | "Different job: homing to a named thing you have already seen this session, fully on-device, no one else watching the camera." |
| "Works anywhere / finds anything / never wrong" | Overtrust from confident outputs is a documented harm ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [calibrated honesty](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830398.3830496)) | "Works for a short baked word list and objects in view; if it loses the target it says so and never invents a distance." |
| "No more asking for help" | Asking for help can itself be an independent living skill; the problem is loss of control ([CSCW 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3449223)) | "Fewer moments where this one job depends on whoever happens to be nearby." |
| "User-tested with the blind" (if untrue) | Parachute-design criticism ([ACM Interactions](https://interactions.acm.org/archive/view/january-february-2023/visualization-for-the-blind)) | "Built by sighted hackathon team; next step is paid, BLV-led testing." |

**What a 30-second demo must never imply**

- That it finds an object never seen this session, or finds arbitrary nouns beyond the baked list ([CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107), [Find My Things](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Morrison-Find-My-Things-ASSETS2023.pdf)).
- That it sees stairs, obstacles, traffic or people, or that ticks continue as a trusted guide after perception is dead ([NavCog3](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/2018/01/p270-sato.pdf), [ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546)).
- That the wearer can hold the phone/cane normally while reaching - the hands-free claim must be visibly true ([ICHI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3234695.3236345)).
- That a spoken scene inventory is the product; it is explicitly not the v1 job ([Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91755-w)).
- The `slay percept` → HOLD → LOST step should be shown, not described: it is the honest-failure evidence judges can hear ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [UIST 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3746059.3747597)).

## Open questions (cannot answer without BLV testers)

1. **Can a cane user follow spoken bearings while walking** without losing track of traffic/ambient sound? Which output - bone conduction, one-ear headset, chest speaker - is acceptable in a real hall? ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2511.00945))
2. **What does "lost" need to sound like?** Is a distinct earcon enough, or is a spoken phrase required? Is HOLD useful or confusing? How fast should LOST come after stale frames? ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546))
3. **Which spatial words work?** "Two metres, a little left" vs clock-face vs "three steps ahead"; and do BLV users want metres at all on a tabletop? ([object search study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13467581.2026.2709913), [AppleVis](https://www.applevis.com/blog/apple-airtag-review-simple-setup-use-deliver-super-easy-accurate-finding-misplaced-items))
4. **Which objects and names matter most**, and what synonyms do real wearers use? Does "keys" fire for their actual keychain? What personal items should the 16–32-noun list include? ([CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713107), [Find My Things](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Morrison-Find-My-Things-ASSETS2023.pdf))
5. **How should multiple same-class objects be disambiguated** ("two cups - the blue one"), given personalization and cultural misfit findings? ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546))
6. **Is the tick mapping learnable in 30 seconds?** Does stereo pan plus rate work for an off-axis hand, or does the "finding phase" failure appear? ([CHI 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790782))
7. **Does the chest mount actually see a tabletop and a hand entering from below** across body sizes, clothing and postures - and does the wearer find it comfortable or stigmatizing? ([Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91755-w) on stigma/cost barriers)
8. **Floor drops vs tabletops:** is a chest camera enough for the dropped-medication job, or must that be explicitly out of scope? ([Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91755-w))
9. **What correction affordance is needed?** Wording and timing for "repeat," "wrong one," "stop," and how much verification the wearer wants before trusting a homing cue. ([ASSETS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546), [UIST 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3746059.3747597))
10. **Does on-device processing fully resolve the privacy concern**, or do wearers still worry about a camera worn on the chest recording bystanders? ([CHI 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3587281.3587296), [SOUPS](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/soups2019posters-akter.pdf))
11. **Would a short retrace be trusted at all**, and under what error rate would participants abandon it? ([NavCog](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kkitani/pdf/AGKITA-MHCI16.pdf), [trust review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10554181251355426))
